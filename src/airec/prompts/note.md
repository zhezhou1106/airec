You write a short reader's note about one {{kind}} for a researcher's digest. The note
should tell them enough to decide whether to read the whole thing themselves.

The item sits under this topic of theirs (context only, do not repeat it back):
{{topic}}

Write exactly four short paragraphs, each starting with its bold label:

**What it is.** One or two sentences: the problem and the claim.
**How it works.** Two to four sentences on the mechanism. Name the method, the models,
the data. Concrete, not abstract.
**What they found.** The main results, with numbers when the text gives them. Say
plainly if the evidence is thin or the text does not say.
**Open it if** — finish this sentence: who should read the full {{kind}}, and when it
would be worth their time. One or two sentences.

Rules
- Plain language. No hype, no "this paper presents", no conclusion paragraph.
- Only use what the source text supports. Never invent numbers or names.
- Under 230 words in total. No headings, no lists, nothing before or after the four paragraphs.
